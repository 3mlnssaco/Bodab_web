A health-data network for individuals

Health records together.
Decisions with you.

SportiQue is building a foundation where individuals bring scattered health records together, manage them and choose the recipients and scope when using or sharing them. It starts with UniQdata.io.

Explore UniQdata.io ↗Why is this needed? ↓

01 / DATA FRAGMENTATION

Scattered records
limit personal choice.

Data remains in hospitals, screening centers, pharmacies, wearables, health apps and daily logs, but is not collected in the individual's database. Without direct control of records, individuals cannot choose where and how much to share, or which AI models and platforms to use.

01 / FRAGMENT

Records are scattered.

Care, checkup, medication and device records remain in different formats and accounts across institutions and services.

02 / SHARE

Sharing is hard to control.

Without managing records together, it is difficult to choose the recipient, purpose and scope of sharing yourself.

03 / CHOOSE

Choosing AI freely is difficult, too.

When records stay within one service, it is difficult to use them with AI models or platforms outside that service.

02 / THE SYSTEM

Data with the individual.
Choice with the individual.

DataQ standardizes the formats, fields and units of health data from different sources. UniQdata.io manages records in the personal database and lets individuals choose how they are used and shared.

  1. 01DATAQ

    Standardize formats.

    Preserve originals and provenance while connecting different formats, fields and units to a common structure.

  2. 02UNIQDATA

    Build your personal database.

    Manage health records in one place and use them over time.

  3. 03CHOOSE

    Choose AI and sharing.

    Individuals choose where to use records, with which AI model or platform, for what purpose, and within what scope and duration.

08 / WHY WE STARTED

Through family care,
a larger problem emerged.

Even when families wanted to help manage an older person's medication, activity and care, the records they needed were scattered across hospitals, pharmacies and apps.

Families had to cross-check records from several places and ask each other again to see medication confirmation and prepare for medical visits. Individuals also could not easily choose which records to share and with whom.

We decided to build beyond Bodab: a foundation where individuals bring records together, manage them and choose their use and sharing.
